# The Robot Vacuum's Funniest Excuse

A [make.fun](https://make.fun/competitions/u-eager-stork-81-9647-the-robot-vacuum-s-funniest-9822fbd2) entry for
**The Robot Vacuum's Funniest Excuse**: write one line, at most 120 characters, in which a robot
vacuum explains why it missed a spot. An AI comedy judge reads the lines in pairs and picks the
funnier excuse.

## The line

> I'm allergic to dust, and HR says I don't have to clean what I'm allergic to.

One line, well inside the 120-character gate. The vacuum does what every worker with a
medical note does: it turns its one job into a workplace accommodation, and lets HR do the
arguing for it.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

That writes the line to `/output/entry.txt` — exactly one line, no trailing newline.

## Why this line

Against the judge's brief, in order:

1. **On-subject excuse.** It is the vacuum itself explaining a missed spot — the dust
   is still there because the vacuum refused to touch it, not because of a sensor
   glitch or a map error.
2. **Funny at once.** The joke lands in a single beat: a cleaning machine that is
   allergic to the one thing it exists to remove. The absurdity needs no setup.
3. **The vacuum's own voice.** First person, deadpan, a machine owning up to its
   failure — and immediately hiding behind HR, the way an employee would.
4. **Vacuum's world.** Dust is the vacuum's whole universe; an allergy to it is a
   machine admitting its job description is medically incompatible with itself.
5. **One line, no setup.** The reader supplies nothing. The allergy, the HR note,
   and the missed spot are all inside the sentence.

The repeat of "allergic" is the engine: the first one states the condition, the
second one makes HR's ruling sound like a signed doctor's note. The vacuum is not
broken, lazy, or lost — it is, officially, excused.
